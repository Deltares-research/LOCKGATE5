
Theory
===========

KASGOLF can be used to calculate the hydraulic head over an opened lockgate. The hydraulic head is the difference between the water level in the lock chamber and the water level in the gate recess. This head difference is driven by a passing wave generated by a ship that sails in or out of the lock. Through vertical openings on the sides of the gate and a horinzontal opening underneath the gate, water flows between the lock chamber and gate recess ('deurkas' in Dutch). These flows are calculated using the energy conservation law in open channel flow. As a result, a translatory wave will propagate through the gate recess affecting the head difference over the gate. The gate is considered as an infinitely thin plate, since processes within the vertical openings have minimal influence on the characteristics of the translatory wave in the gate recess and therefore can be neglected. A top view of an opened lock with propagating wave is shown in the figure below:

.. image:: ../images/overzicht_kas_kolk.png

For the calculation of these water levels, the length of the gate is divided in 10 equal sections (both channels are half and together count as 1, see the figure below) with each section having a water level on both the lock chamber and gate recess sides. By averaging the water levels in the sections, the average water level on both sides of the gate is calculated that can be translated to the head difference over the gate. Friction on the translatory wave in the gate recess is neglected in KASGOLF.

.. image:: ../images/overzicht_kas_kolk_secties.png

To run a simulation with KASGOLF, the bed level of the lock and the dimensions of the gate, gate recess and openings need to be inserted in the model. The wave height can be inserted as a time array for location A. After a certain delay, this wave height then reaches location B. The user can choose between a delay based on shallow wave theory (water depth dependent) or the ship's sailing velocity, provided it exceeds the shallow wave propagation speed. The propagation velocity of the translatory wave in gate recess is calculated using shallow wave theory. If the second option is chosen, the sailing velocity that is inserted has to be equal to or exceed the wave propagation velocity according to shallow wave theory otherwise an error notification will come up that provides you with the minimum required value for sailing velocity to use this feature. Reason for that is, the code is not able to deal with wave seperation from the ship's surface that occurs when the ship's sailing velocity is lower than the propagation velocity according to shallow wave theory.
